## Owning-layer defects and bounded repair

The document owns one optical program/context. The old initializer discarded the current program, buffer, textures and prepared source on every neutral/tint switch. The public demo's inactive Bold and selected Italic therefore recompiled repeatedly as their shared source updated. The original algorithm fixture had separate programs per control and did not expose that interleaving. The repair caches only the four finite static/contact × neutral/tint programs within the same context, with program-specific uniform locations and attributes, shared current textures, and complete invalidation/cleanup. It does not cache surface admission or stale paint leases.

The original tint map reduced source RGB to one linear-light luminance scalar. Dark source detail then collapsed under 8-bit quantization. In the WC controlled pair, the same source revision 57 produced 148 neutral content colors but only three selected content colors; selected contrast remained safe at 6.429764174353648. The repair retains clamped per-channel perceptual source detail, then applies the original 0.68/0.32 envelope in linear light. Each output channel remains on the fallback fill's original safe side. It neither adds noise nor changes the backdrop to manufacture variation.

These cache costs can delay real media-query event delivery under load, but the retained artifact does not contain enough preference timing data to establish the light-case cause conclusively. Real preference-resolution tests with synthetic MediaQueryList events show that live changed preferences also reject a late held decode and repaint statically before its change event is delivered. The repair therefore preserves the original one-second static gate rather than labeling the failure harmless or increasing its timeout.
